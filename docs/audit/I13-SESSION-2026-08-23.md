### Top usability failures, in severity order

1. **A blocking failure with no message.** Hull Studio renders nothing and
   says nothing. No on-screen error, no logs, no "geometry unavailable"
   state. This is the whole finding: the failure to COMMUNICATE is a separate
   and more serious defect than whatever the underlying bug is.
2. **No request logging.** The participant's own first instinct — "can you
   check logs?" — had nothing to check. `Handler.log_message` is silenced.
3. **The only error channel is one line of small mono text under the canvas**,
   resting value an em dash. Invisible to anyone not told about it.
4. **Diagnosis required source-reading and curl.** Beyond any non-expert.
5. **The mission read-back silently disagreed with the brief** on four of six
   lines (category, cruise speed, crew, battery) and the participant's
   reaction to it was never captured, because they were blocked before it
   mattered.

### What went RIGHT, and should not be lost

- The read-back screen EXISTS at all, and exists explicitly because the parser
  is known to fail silently. That is the correct design instinct.
- The air-draft tile declares a hole in the product, names the silent misread
  ("'3 m total height' -> lwl_hint_m = 3.0"), and states what would close it.
  A tool that volunteers its own defects is rare and valuable.
- `/api/manifest` exposes all 17 genome parameters; nothing is hidden.
