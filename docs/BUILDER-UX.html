The Lofting FloorFour phases, one honest boat

A UX for DIY builders who have never heard of a prismatic coefficient — designed against what the Naval-AI backend actually computes today, not against what a boat-design app is supposed to look like. Every component names the field or endpoint it binds to. Where the backend cannot answer, the interface says so instead of drawing a gauge.

The primitive everything is built from

Before any screen: every quantity carries {value, tier, sigma, basis}. A live evaluation returns GM_m = {0.82, L1, σ 0.272, measured} next to what used to be freeboard_m = {0.842, L1, σ 0.02, assumed}. Same tier, same colour if you are careless — and one was computed from the hull while the other's sigma had been typed into the server by hand. That one is fixed (§06); the discipline it taught is not optional.

So the atomic component is a Quantity badge, and the rule that makes it honest is sharper than it looks:

The single most important line in this document

Colour on basis, not on tier. A traffic light that reads tier alone renders freeboard_m (σ hard-coded 0.02) and the solar pair (σ typed as ×0.25 and ×0.35 in the server) as confident L1 green. They are not measurements. And SIGMA_PLACEHOLDER — which the energy module documents as "no input sigma was supplied — DO NOT USE" — must render grey, never green.

01

Mission Builder

Intent → a legal envelope, not a boat

This phase does not produce a design. It produces a box — a bounded region the rest of the app may not leave. The governance kernel compiles a constitution into parameter bounds and extra constraint rows, so a length ceiling becomes a slider limit rather than a rejection the user meets an hour later.

The measured case for doing it this way is the strongest number in the project: sampling without the compiled box produced roundness > 0 on 60 of 60 draws and the unroller refused all 60 — the search was ranking a design space that was 100% unbuildable in plywood. With the box, 30 of 30 unrolled.

Free text alone is unsafe — measured against your own example brief

The parser is keyword-based and honest about it, but it fails silently in ways that matter. On "…1-ton battery payload, 3m total height…": "3m" is swallowed as waterline length (lwl_hint_m = 3.0) — there is no air-draft concept anywhere in the tree — and "1-ton battery" becomes target displacement, not payload, because no prose path sets PayloadSpec.mass_kg. "Monohull" and "wave-piercer" are not parsed at all. So free text seeds the form; it never commits it. Every parsed value is shown back for confirmation, and anything unrecognised is surfaced as prose in a "we didn't understand this" list, not dropped.

Binds to

The "was / is now" bars are grammar.PARAMS against CompiledPolicy.box(category). Every move is already recorded as a BoxEdit(param, edge, was, now, source) — the why it narrowed list is that tuple, rendered. The ⚑ extra checks are CompiledPolicy.rows, appended to the eight base constraints.

Motion design, derived from an invariant

A policy may only append a row and only move a bound inward; a floor that would loosen a limits.py bar raises at compile time, and so does one exactly equal to it. So the envelope animation can only ever close in from the ends — never widen, never jump. Let the motion express that. It teaches something true.

Build note for step 4

A Constitution is not free to synthesise from a form. Every field must be a PolicyValue carrying value, unit, source, basis, and construction rejects a source string under 20 characters. That is a deliberate bar. The form must map each choice to a real, pre-written provenance sentence — not echo the user's click back as justification.

Parametric Studio

Six behavioural sliders over a 16-parameter grammar

Today's ui/index.html builds one slider per grammar parameter, labelled "Block coeff" and "TARGET prismatic coefficient". That is the engineer's surface. It stays — behind a switch — and the builder gets six controls that each drive several parameters along a curated path.

The wave-piercing bow cannot be drawn, and the repo already knows it

There is no stem-rake gene. The form library records the hole by name — "stem rake, bow overhang or a counter stern: LOA == LWL by construction" — and catalogues both wave_piercing_monohull and axe_bow as not expressible and excluded. The plumb_stem feature is marked expressible for the wrong reason: the grammar cannot make anything else.

So ship Bow attitude across the range the engine can generate, and render the reverse-rake zone as a locked extension labelled "needs a new hull parameter — not buildable yet." Never draw a bow the engine cannot produce; the user will design around it and the export will refuse.

Component behaviour

ComponentBehaviour
Behavioural slider Fires on every input event, no debounce. evaluate() measures 11.35 ms warm, and there is a CI-enforced 50 ms ceiling on it plus a <100 ms gate on every widget — so the budget is defended by tests, not by hope. Track ends are the policy-compiled bounds, so illegal is undraggable. A faint ghost track past each end shows the ungoverned range with the reason on hover: it teaches instead of blocking silently.
Two-rate render loop The key architectural call, and the measurements make it easy: panel_mesh() is 1.66 ms — live at 60 fps alongside the physics. closed_mesh() is 49.8 ms — regenerate on change, not during drag. unroll.hull_panels() is 869 ms — export-time only, never in the loop. So: draw the fast mesh while dragging, settle to the fine one on release, and unroll only when the user asks to build.
Buildability meter Sits directly beneath the sliders that break it. Amber as refold error climbs, red when the hull leaves the kit corner. This placement is the entire point: 3° of flare alone moves refold error from ~4.7 mm to 26.8 mm against a 5 mm bar. The user has to feel that in the same gesture that causes it, or they will meet it as a wall in Phase 4.
Locked slider Bottom shape carries a padlock at the rounded-bilge end with the reason inline. It is not greyed out — it still moves across its legal span. This is the compiled box made visible: the policy clipped roundness to exactly [0, 0], and the padlock is that fact.
Capsize check A deliberate, explicit action — not a live light — because the GZ curve floats the hull at 16 heels from 0–60° by bisection and costs about a second. It returns real large-angle stability: maximum righting arm, the angle of vanishing stability, area under the curve to 30°, and deck-edge immersion. Present it as "we heeled her over — here's where she stops coming back", with the assumptions listed (watertight to the sheer, trim held fixed, no declared downflooding openings).
Feature toggles Deck features are mass objects, not hull modifications — see the caveat below, which is sharper than it first appears.
Expert switch Swaps the six behavioural sliders for the raw sixteen from GET /bounds. Same evaluation, same constraint rows, no second code path. This is what makes the abstraction trustworthy rather than a wall.
The starboard platform — two blockers, and one is not the one you'd guess

Geometry: the manufacturing model emits shell parts at qty = 2, port and starboard mirrored. An asymmetric hull feature breaks that at the panel level, and the arrangement module has no path into nesting at all.

Physics — fixed 2026-08-21, and it was one seam. This section previously said the toggle could not recalculate the asymmetric weight balance, because PayloadSpec had no transverse coordinate and tcg was identically zero in production. Everything downstream was already built and tested — MassItem.y_m → aggregate.tcg_m → atan(TCG/GM) → the list row — and nothing joined it up. Two opt-in seams close it: PayloadSpec.y_frac_bwl and evaluate(extra_mass_items=…). MEASURED: one tonne at 0.30·BWL to starboard now reads TCG 0.160 m, list 12.14°, and the row fails — where it previously read exactly −2.000 on every hull ever evaluated. So the toggle can tell the truth about heel. The geometry blocker above still stands: it is a deck object, not a panel.

03

Reality Check

Eight constraint rows, three human questions

The inequality vector has exactly eight rows — freeboard · gm · bend_radius · trim · list · lcb · proportions · rules — and that is the complete set of things this platform can fail a design on. Do not invent a ninth. Group them under the questions a builder actually asks, in the order the fear arrives.

Question one

Will it float and stay upright?

freeboard · gm · trim · list

Largest, top, always visible. Plus the capsize check on demand.

Question two

Can I actually build this?

bend_radius · proportions

Plus the refold meter that decides kit versus mould. Mirrored into the studio rail.

Question three

Is it legal, and what if I sell it?

rules · lcb

ISO 12215 scantlings, RCD routing. An assessment aid — never a certification claim.

Component behaviour

ComponentBehaviour
Constraint light Not a dot — a headroom bar with the limit printed. A bar says how close, which is what links a slider to its consequence. Colour is never the only channel: ● measured, ◆ assumed, ▲ lower-bound, ▨ absent, plus text. list was in the absent register until 2026-08-21 and now reads as a real measurement — see the platform note below.
Waterline attitude The hull in the viewport is the trim indicator. The backend solves sinkage and trim simultaneously — a damped 2-D Newton warm-started from the level float, which is what keeps it inside the 50 ms budget. Move weight, the boat settles to a new attitude, live. The numeric readout confirms; it does not lead.
Mass-closure bar Non-negotiable. A baseline resolves 45% of displacement to named items and leaves 3298 kg unaccounted. Every stability light above is computed on that estimate. Four confident green lights without this bar beneath them would be the exact dishonesty the platform exists to prevent. Unresolved mass is listed, never absorbed.
Power balance A 24-hour band, not a dial — a dial implies a steady state, and solar is a day shape. The builder's real question is whether they end the day up or down. Draw is properly propagated (σ from resistance and drivetrain in quadrature); generation is not — it is a flat daily average with a typed ±25%. Say so in the caption, in the user's words. There is no state-of-charge model, so never animate a battery filling and emptying: that would imply a simulation that does not exist.
Froude guard The drag model is valid to Fn 0.45. Past it the result is badged invalid and its uncertainty inflates to the size of the answer — the model disowning itself. Semi-displacement and planing are refused by name; there is no Savitsky-class model in the tree. So the speed control carries a hard stop with the reason, and the gauge shows REFUSED, never a number. For a 16 m hull that ceiling is 11.0 knots; at your 6-knot cruise (Fn 0.25) you are comfortably inside it.
Chop tile Renders as absent — and the honest copy is better than a flat "not supported", because the parts genuinely exist: the sea-state preset for Black Sea inshore is literally a 1 m chop, and heave response can be computed. They are not wired together, and slamming pressure is uncalibrated by a factor of four. This tile is the one that proves the others are telling the truth.
Provenance strip Persistent, quiet, at the foot. One sentence naming what the physics was checked against and where the user sits relative to it. Links to the evidence rather than restating it.
The one backend change that unlocks the most UX

Phase 3's headline interaction — drag a battery, watch the waterline — is 90% built and blocked by a seam. The equilibrium solver, the mass aggregate with quadrature sigma, and even an arrangement-side producer of positioned mass items all exist and are tested. They cannot reach each other: evaluate() takes no item list and builds one internally from hard-coded position fractions, with the battery pinned at 0.45 × LWL. Add an items: parameter plus a transverse coordinate — on the order of thirty lines — and both the drag interaction and the list light become real.

04

Builder's Output

From design tool to build project

As of 2026-08-21 this phase has a real LANE, and a retracted result. The pipeline runs end to end — mission sentence → governed search → buildability refinement → a complete DXF whose every BOM part is drawn (a check that caught 102 of 186 parts missing from an earlier cut file). Ninety plywood sheets at 75.2% nest, an ISO-derived 18 mm bottom, 141 kg of epoxy, a 207-line BOM. An hour earlier the same lane wrote a DXF for panels that missed the hull by 221.5 mm with nothing objecting.

Retracted the same day it was written — and PU-4 is why

This paragraph previously reported a delivered hull at "4.9 and 4.8 mm, header stamped REFOLD VERIFIED". That hull is not buildable. Re-measured as a FAMILY rather than at one station count: n=41 → 4.92 mm · n=81 → 5.22 mm · n=161 → 8.71 mm — RISING, verdict NON_DEVELOPABLE.

The refinement search had been scored on the 41-station number, so it optimised the sagitta artefact this document's own §04 identifies rather than the curvature: a surface with Gaussian curvature 7.8e-14 reads 17.1 mm at n=41. It gamed the metric. export_dxf now gates on refold_convergence's trend and REFUSES that hull by name, which is PU-4 enforced at the boundary instead of stated as an intention. The kit route in the wireframe below is therefore still a search with no guaranteed answer — as it is drawn.

So the interface changes character here. Sliders disappear. And it opens not with a download button but with a routing verdict, because the backend makes one: every hull is classified sheet-kit or mould, and the DXF exporter raises rather than writing a file when the panels do not re-fold within the bar.

That refusal is a first-class outcome and must be met before the user reaches for a download.

The routing question was being asked at the wrong resolution — MEASURED 2026-08-21

A refold deviation is measured against a panel built as straight chords between the hull's stations, and the ladder discretises at 41 of them — a count chosen for hydrostatics cost, by nothing to do with manufacturing. Part of every reported deviation is therefore that 40-segment polyline's sagitta. On a hull whose Gaussian curvature is machine-zero it is all of it:

hullndev_fracn=41n=81n=161n=321reading
flare 00.00000017.15.84.11.5falls → discretisation

flare 25°0.275547379.8448.3484.6503.6rises → real double curvature

So the verdict is the direction, not the value — the same discipline the project already applies to a CFD mesh family, where one grid is a number and a family is a verdict. A shortfall that falls under refinement is measurement; one that rises is geometry, and only the second is a reason to change the boat. unroll.refold_convergence() now returns PASSES / REFINING / NON_DEVELOPABLE / REFUSED on exactly that test.

This also bears on the question the repo left open — whether the grammar admits a sub-bar hull at all. It does, and the stronger answer came from a parallel search the same day: a hull measuring 4.952 mm at the ladder's own 41 stations with the full ladder passing and GM +2.545 m. The kit route is real.

But it is a trade, and the trade is what the UI must show. Developability wants narrow and flat; stability wants beam. An unconstrained refold search reached 3.900 mm on a hull with GM −0.35 m — it capsizes. Against the lowest-energy hull from the same brief, the buildable one costs 59 → 121 plywood sheets, 1825 → 3679 build hours, 412 → 595 Wh/nm. That is a product decision for the builder to make with their eyes open, not a defect to hide behind a one-tap "fix it" button — which is why the screen below now shows both boats and their bill.

Component behaviour

ComponentBehaviour
Route verdict First on the screen, before any artefact. Kit or mould, with the number and the bar. Never a silent fallback, never a download that turns out to be useless.
The cuttable search A job with progress and a cancel — never a control. MEASURED: only 3 of 400 random hulls clear the 5 mm bar, and a local search seeded 120 mm away did not reach it in 900 s. The recipe that works is a 76 420-draw sweep (most die in the L0 gate at 0.27 ms) followed by an evolutionary refine. Drawing that as a toggle would promise something instant that takes minutes, and would hide the trade below. The result screen shows both boats and the bill.
Blame bars The refusal ranks which slider cost how many millimetres and offers a one-tap return to the kit corner — flare 0, forefoot 0, deadrise warp within about 8° — with the cost stated in knots and interior volume, previewed before it is accepted. A refusal that tells you what to change is guidance; one that does not is a wall.
Download gate The ZIP unlocks only when the round trip passes: every panel present, panels re-fold within the bar, the DXF re-parses, units are declared in the file, and thickness was derived from the ISO scantling rule rather than typed. Each check is visible and individually explained — a checklist the builder can read, not a disabled button. This is enforceable server-side, not a UI promise: the exporter already raises.
Nesting canvas Pan/zoom sheets, part labels, per-sheet and overall utilisation. Sheet count is counted off the layout, never estimated from area — and the copy says which kind of number it is, because the builder is about to buy plywood with it. Separate bins per thickness: you cannot cut a 21 mm bottom from a 15 mm sheet.
Two BOMs, separately closed The structural kit derives from geometry; systems (motor, batteries, solar) form a second list with its own closure. Each shows mass closure and cost closure as measured fractions. Missing lines are named — today that is glass cloth, fasteners and copper stitching wire, none of which the BOM engine emits, and epoxy is a single 1.4 kg/m² scalar with no split between fillets, coats and sheathing. A low closure number is a finding, displayed.
Assembly stage Honest but thin: a sequence derived from sheet assignment. Stitch-hole spacing, wire placement and puzzle joints do not exist in the backend, and the stage says so rather than illustrating a process the file does not encode. Scarph joints are modelled — as material allowance and split location, not as cut taper — and the note must distinguish the two, because a builder cutting to the drawn line would get it wrong.
Legal stage Not a checkbox to skip. Full screen, plain language, clause text verbatim: building for your own use puts you outside the Recreational Craft Directive for five years from putting it into service, and selling inside that window triggers post-construction assessment by a notified body — the most expensive moment to need one. If routing lands on notified body required, the release refuses to emit. Framed throughout as an assessment aid that cites clauses and routes work, and is not legal advice.
05

Responsive, and one honest amputation

  • ≥1280 px — full studio. Three zones as drawn. This is where design happens.
  • 768–1279 px — viewport first. Shape and vitals become bottom sheets over a full-bleed 3D view, one open at a time. The buildability meter stays pinned to the shape sheet; separating it from the sliders defeats its purpose.
  • <768 px — no studio. Phones get the Mission Builder, a read-only design viewer, the full Reality Check and the Build dashboard including download. The sliders are not offered. Nobody lofts a boat on a phone, and a degraded control surface at that size produces hulls someone then has to unpick on a laptop. Say it plainly: "Shaping needs a bigger screen — here's what you've drawn so far."

Accessibility falls out of the honesty contract rather than being bolted on: state is carried in shape and text as well as colour (● measured, ◆ assumed, ▲ lower-bound, ▨ absent); numbers use tabular figures so columns align; sliders are keyboard-operable with arrow steps matched to each parameter's real resolution; and the viewport publishes a live text summary of the current hull for screen readers.

Where the ORDER lives — not here

This page is the design: what the builder sees, and what each control binds to. It carries no schedule, no owner and no sequence, deliberately. Those are docs/BUILD-PLAN.md §PU — The builder-facing surface, which is the one plan and holds PU-1 … PU-7 with their gates and their done-when. A second ordered list here would be the defect this project keeps finding: a thing declared twice, drifting apart the moment one copy is edited.

The phase's bar is gap I13, already open and already worded for it — a recorded non-expert session producing a hull that passes the full ladder. It cannot be met with a screenshot.

The through-line

Four screens, one idea: the interface hides the mathematics and refuses to hide the uncertainty. A builder never sees a prismatic coefficient. They do see that 3298 kg is unaccounted for, that the drag model was validated on a ship nothing like theirs, that nobody has predicted how their bow behaves in a chop, and that their hull may want a mould rather than a flat-pack. Hiding any of those would make a smoother demo and a worse boat — and this platform's whole thesis is that a failing check is information, never something to soften.
